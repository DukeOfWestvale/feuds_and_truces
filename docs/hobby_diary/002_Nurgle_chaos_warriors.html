Episode 002: Nurgle Marked Warriors of Chaos

Getting back into Warhammer Fantasy

Warhammer
Painting

Introduction

Without a doubt my favourite miniatures I’ve worked on, both for nostalgia and the paintjob I accomplished, are the Warhammer Fantasy 6th edition Chaos Knights. I painted a unit of five of these late in 2024 and I think they are just superb - everything I ever wanted out of Warhammer when I was a kid just getting into the hobby without much money. They form the core of my growing Warhammer fantasy chaos army and I’m planning to do a dedicated historical post about them at some point in the future. Briefly, this army is an homage to all the things I saw and loved about the release of Warriors of Chaos for Warhammer Fantasy’s 6th edition, sometime around 2001 - 2002. A big part of what I recall are Paul Sawyer’s White Dwarf articles (my first encounter with the ‘slow-grow’ format, something beloved by many people) which were mostly grounded and gritty, in black armour and gold trim. I also want to bring in a few other elements such as the more ‘chaotic’ nature that was present in some of the older editions, and to try and capture a human element (both by showing the tragic humanity of Chaos Warriors and contrasting that with the in-human nature of many of the models). This article is all about how the newest additions to the army touch on those chaotic and human aspects: a unit of Nurgle marked Chaos Warriors and a Nurgle Marked Sorcerer on Steed.

Warriors of Chaos with the mark of Nurgle

Sometime in late 2024 I saw a sales post for some metal (definitely genuine, 100% not recast) Warhammer Fantasy chaos models for a very reasonable (almost too good to be true) price. I picked up two units that came from pre-sixth edition - one of which was a unit of Tzeentch marked warriors and the other a unit of Nurgle marked warriors. Being pre-sixth edition the models have much more of a chaotic nature than the uniformed, armoured forces that were typical of sixth edition chaos warriors and this was initially a bit jarring for me but it presented an interesting challenge. One of the first things I did hobby-wise on arriving in Luxembourg was start to base the unit of Nurlge marked warriors. This was more difficult than I expected due to the old models being built for ‘slotta bases’, which a metal bar that connects below the feet of the model. I didn’t have slotta bases (in fact I had cheap mdf bases) so I experimented with cutting/snapping off the bar (works well for some models but not others), carving hole in the mdf board (difficult, don’t recommend), and building up a base of stone and cork that could disguise the attached bar. On the plus side, these models didn’t require assembly and being made of metal I didn’t even attempt to tackle the mold lines. All in all, not too bad. The other unit I added was the Nurlge Marked Chaos Sorcrer on Steed. Direct from Sixth edition this model was one I recognised from White Dwarf and it was one I had been really excited to add to my collection when I saw it available. It’s absolutely excellent, and one of three character models I have acquired from that period that I love. I was missing a weapon hand and gave it a suitably rusted, pitted, wickedly curved sword from the Cursed City skeleton sergeant, which works perfectly as a cavalry sabre (not that I expect him to be swinging it at anyone, but we will see). I also added a crow, again from Curse City, sitting on the back of the horse behind the rider to balance how it looks from a profile view (the torse splits the model mostly in two, but between the horses’s head and the raise weapon arm I wanted something behind the rider). Aside from that the model went together easily, I think I filled the gaps with PVA glue since I didnt’ have any greenstuff at the time. The base was made from glueing two 25mm mdf bases together.

Painting

Painting these models took quite a while and I actually painted them off and on, taking a big break between finishing the models and coming back to basing them (and doing some touch-ups to the Sorcerer). I was struggling with not having exactly the paints I wanted, not having anywhere to prime the models with a rattlecan (apartment living is a new experience), not specific on my vision (where they fit on the continuum between black metal sixth edition and wild colours of previous chaos warriors). Mostly they were a mix of contrast paints and a few other bits and pieces, loosly following the recipe I used for the Chaos Knights which comes from Koltti’s Patreon and being very free to add colour within the pink-green-orange triangle that I associate with Nurgle. I really like how some of these miniatures have tiny nurglings resting on their shoulders or helmets and it hints at the more ‘goofy’ aspect of Warhammer that I think I missed when I was younger. Another reason I struggled to get the warriors finished is that I realised I only had eight suitable models, which wasn’t enough to form a proper unit. I knew at some point I’d need to find some additions, probably through heavily converting some models or using unit fillers. At the time I wasn’t really ready to jump into that, so the eight warriors were put aside as soon as they were painted to a good enough standard.

I then switched my attention to the Sorcerer. I started out making the armour quite yellow (yellow being the extreme ‘highlight’ of green), and focused on making the horse a mixture of orange and pink (not explicitely orange and pink, which would have been ridiculous, but using browns and other colours mixed with those colours to give hints of orange and pink). I won’t pretend I had planned it out this way, I was just painting instinctively, but conceptually I think this works: I’ve basically managed to complete the purple-green-orange triangle again, but using very different colours. It was about this time that I took the long break mentioned above because I wasn’t really happy with how the model was looking. After my long break I recently went back to the model and significantly darkened the armour and made a few other small changes, and I’m now much happier.

Basing and story-telling

With the models themselves painted it was time to focus on the bases. While I do leave the bases until last they are by no means least. In fact in terms of story-telling and narrative, the bases are going to do fully half of the heavy lifting. When I first painted the Chaos Knights I realised that Chaos was at it’s most frightening not as some distant threat in the hellscape Chaos wastes, but as a very present threat to every-day humans (and above I mentioned that portraying humanity is a key element in this project). I imagined the moment of terror where the farmers and villagers look up to see the silhouettes of mounted, armoured warriors cresting the hilltop and knew that this was what I wanted to represent with this army. The bases of this army should invoke that idea of farmland or forest border. To make my bases I use a mixture of coffee grounds, dirt, small stones, and my secret ingredient: breadcrumbs. I don’t quite remember how I got onto breadcrumbs but I know it involved putting some bread in the oven for too long and then running it through a blender, then instead of it going in the meal I was making it made it into my stash of basing material, and I’ve used it ever since. Larger chunks of breadcrumb look a lot like pitted rocks to me, and they add a lot of variety. Once the material is on I paint everything with a mix of three greens, which in this case was Plaguebearer contrast as the base, Mortarian Grime contrast as the shade and Army Painter Necrotic flesh as the highlight. Using contrast is very helpful here as this is all mixed via wet blending. After that has dried I use PVA glue which gets applied directly in some places and watered down in others (skipping some areas of the base completely), and use this to stick on some static grass (“Battlefield Grass Green” from Army Painter). I’m careful to hold the model upside down and tap the bases so that at least some of the grass stands upright by way of gravity (I think there are proper applicator devices that do this better but I don’t have one of those).

Once this has dried (mostly) I go in with a mix of contrast colours. This does two things: first adding a whole load of variety in colour and shade and I apply a general rule of making the colours darker underneath the model to give an appearance of shadow. Second, the contrast soaks into the static grass and works to flatten it down, which adds variety in the level of height or depth of the grass. Overall, a really nice effect for not too much effort and this is a perfectly fine place to stop.

There are two final things to add after this which really add to the bases. The first are tiny leaves made by a leaf punch, bought from Games Workshop/Greenstuff world. I use real leaves as the material, ideally from different trees for colour and texture variation, and stick these on with watered down PVA, then later spash some of the contrast paints onto them. The last thing, which I haven’t used here but will add later, are tufts of grass. These were always in the ‘nice but probably too expensive’ category for me but I picked some up and reserve them for this project.

When working on the base for the Sorcerer I did all the same things except I attempted to tell a small story (I think the term is ‘vignette’, which I really like). I used more Mortarion Grime and Guilliman Flesh, undiluted, around the bases of the horse hooves and also did little pools in approximately the places where the model would have walked. The idea is that the hooves of this model are corrupting the land with every step. It’s tiny and insignificant and I doubt anyone would ever notice this without me telling them, but I know it’s there and it adds character for me.

Final photos

Looking at the models all together I’m happy with the direction. Are they the best thing I’ve ever painted? No. But, they fit with the army, they are interesting, and I’m happy enough. Here you can also see some of the unit fillers I’ve been working on, which were a mix of old models I had from other game systems, some kitbashes (a set of dwarf legs with some third party bits, whom I’m planning to further convert into a standard bearer), and a really big frog (Wilhelm Miniatures sculpt). And finally, a photo of the Sorcerer with the knights. I can see some slight differences, somehow the base looks a lot warmer (maybe that’s because I haven’t done the black trim on the base) but they fit in well enough.

Conclusion

It’s hard to write a conclusion when I know I have more to do on this unit, but I’ve always mentally attached the phrase “slow-grow” to this army so I’m in no rush to do more. Mostly it feels good to have hit a milestone, completing the metal miniatures and the sorcerer to a standard I am happy with. The remaining models, including the big frog (takes up four bases!) unit filler, represents a sort of cross-roads. The models would be non-GW, well outside of my original vision of 6th edition warhammer from the pages of WD, and are also a significant step away from the grounded, realistic look I’ve worked towards so far. Going back to the idea of Chaos warriors as being human and frightening, a cart-sized three-eyed frog is not exactly in line with that (although the Gitrog Monster manages to be both realistic and quite terrifying). The painting style I’ve settled on for this force is still enjoyable and feels right. It’s scratching my itch for a more realistic look without being too grimdark and I’m very happy with it in terms of level of difficulty vs result. Working entirely in acrylic and contrast (and vallejo metals where appropriate) is easy and reduces the level of energy required to get started with any painting session.

In terms of where this project is going I don’t have a strong sense of what I want to work on next and I’m content to test out a few things and see where I end up. I do know that eventually I want to include one unit dedicated to each of the four gods and have a character to lead each, along with a single defining general. One day I’ll add the metal Galrauch model but that’s probably the end-game, which is hopefully a long way off!
